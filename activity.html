Professionalism in Information Technology

Brenan Jay Reconalla  |  BSIT Student

For me, professionalism in Information Technology is not only about being good at coding, fixing computers, or working with networks and databases. It is also about being responsible, respectful, and doing the right thing. While studying this lecture, I learned that IT professionals often work with important information such as personal details, school records, business files, and other data. Because of this, having technical skills is not enough. We also need to know how to use technology properly and responsibly. Being a good IT professional means using our skills in a way that helps people and avoids causing problems.

Among the characteristics of a good IT practitioner, I think respect is one of my strengths. I try to listen to my classmates, teachers, and family when they need help with something related to technology. I also try to explain things in a simple way instead of making someone feel bad for not understanding. However, I know that I still need to improve when it comes to being reliable. Sometimes I underestimate how long a programming activity or school project will take, which leads me to work close to the deadline. I am trying to improve this by managing my time better and starting my tasks earlier. I know that being on time and doing your part is important, especially when working with a group.

Keeping information private is also an important part of being an IT professional. IT workers sometimes have access to passwords, student information, employee records, and other private files. We should not look at or share information that we are not supposed to access. Having access to someone's information does not mean we have the right to use it. We should keep passwords safe, follow the rules of the organization, and ask for permission before sharing private information. This helps protect people and prevents problems caused by the misuse of information.

I can show professionalism in school, especially when working on group programming projects. If we encounter bugs or problems, I should not blame my teammates. Instead, I should focus on my assigned work, communicate with the group, and help find a solution. If I make a mistake in a future job, such as changing something incorrectly in a system or deleting important data, I should be honest about it and inform the person in charge. Hiding the mistake could make the problem worse.

If I noticed that someone was not keeping their password private, I would not try to use their account or share their password with anyone. I would remind them to keep their password safe and not give it to other people. As an IT student, I should understand that passwords and other personal information should be kept private. Simple actions like this can help prevent problems with someone's account.

I also learned that being professional applies to how I act online. As an IT student, I should be careful about what I post, comment, and share on social media. I should avoid posting things that could cause problems for me or other people. I should also be careful when sharing school or work-related information. The way I act online is also part of how people see me, so I need to think before I post something.

Technology is always changing, so I know that I still have a lot to learn. New programming languages, software, cybersecurity issues, and other technologies continue to develop. I want to keep improving my skills even after I graduate. I plan to learn more about programming, cybersecurity, and other areas of IT through school, online resources, and personal projects. I know I will not learn everything at once, but I want to keep learning and improving as I become a better IT professional.

Personal Commitment

As a future IT professional, I commit myself to:

  • 1. Continuous Learning

    I will dedicate myself to earning relevant certifications, reading technical documentation, and staying updated with emerging technologies.

  • 2. Ethical Standards

    I will uphold ethical standards by protecting confidential information, respecting intellectual property, and refusing to engage in software piracy or plagiarism.

  • 3. Accountability and Reliability

    I will practice accountability and reliability by meeting deadlines, communicating honestly with my team, and taking ownership of both my successes and my mistakes.
